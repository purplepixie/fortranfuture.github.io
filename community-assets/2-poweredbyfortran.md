# powered by Fortran

The initial idea for "powered by Fortran" was suggested at the first [Back to the Fortran Future](https://luma.com/ao471jms) (BTTFF) Workshop, a one-day satellite event co-located with RSECon'24, in 2024. Unfortunately, the identity of whoever proposed the inital idea is lost to history. The workshop bought together about 50 people, from the UK, Europe and the USA, to discuss the future of Fortran-based science and engineering. The main themes emerging from the workshop were the need to (re)build the international Fortran community (much of the community is invisible, even to itself), to provide education and training, and to improve tooling to support Fortran-based software engineering.

But people at the workshop also recognised the need to promote, to those _outside_ the Fortran community, the continued use and impact of Fortran in science and engineering. This includes promoting the impact of Fortran to 'end users', i.e., those who benefit from the science and engineering dependent on Fortran codebases but who themselves are not direct users of Fortran or of the Fortran codebases. In the UK, an excellent example is [The Met Office](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/), which provides timely weather forecasts and weather warnings to those in the UK and elsewhere. Weather warnings are immensely valuable to two broad 'end users': society and the economy. Such warnings save lives, reduce potential damage to property, services and resources, and help travel and logistic services re-plan and recover more quickly. Two recent examples of weather warnings in the UK are [Storm Austen](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/about-us/news-and-media/media-centre/weather-and-climate-news/2026/storm-austen-named) and [Storm Boelo](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/about-us/news-and-media/media-centre/weather-and-climate-news/2026/amber-warning-issued-for-storm-boelo-as-storm-austen-moves-through). What few people know is that The Met Office weather warnings (and more generally their weather simulations) are based on very large Fortran codebases (see [here](https://github.com/metoffice)). In other words, and put simply, The Met Office's weather warnings are **powered by Fortran**.

Now that the [FortranFuture Network+](www.fortranfuture.net) project has launched, FortranFuture is in a position to work with others to promote the Fortran ecosystem, i.e., the Fortran language and its related tooling and hardware support, the codebases, the science and engineering, and the services provided to 'end users'. FortranFuture wants to work with others to provide easy and effective ways to promote the Fortran ecosystem. Thus, returning to the initial idea proposed in 2024, there is:
- The concept of "powered by Fortran".
- A set of "powered by Fortran" logos being developed by the [fortran-lang](https://fortran-lang.org/) community.
  - Initial discussions amongst the fortran-lang community are [here](https://fortran-lang.discourse.group/t/powered-by-fortran/11172)
  - Proposed logos developed by Dr Sebastian Mutz are [here](https://github.com/sebastian-mutz/powered_by_fortran_logo)
  - An example of the emerging logo-in-use is [here](https://lnkd.in/p/e4wZPMxQ)
- The hashtag, #poweredbyfortran, e.g., to be used on social media, such as [here](https://lnkd.in/p/eMy3RXvR).
